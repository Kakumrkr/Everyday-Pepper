# Simulated Owner Interviews — Booking App for Salons & Barbers

**What this is:** role-played transcripts using your 13-question script, written as five different real-world owner types would plausibly answer. Vendors, prices and sizes are drawn from how this market commonly looks, but every number here is a **placeholder** — validate with 10 real interviews before you make a build decision on it.

**What this is not:** customer research. Do not quote these numbers in a pitch deck or an investor update.

**How to read it:** each section is one owner speaking in their own words. Numbers they name are written as digits. The scorecard at the end of each interview is the only thing you should carry into a spreadsheet.

## 1. Marcus Webb — barbershop owner, 6 chairs, Birmingham AL

*Owner-operator, 11 years in business, 4 own chairs + 2 booth renters. Answered the phone himself, standing in the back room, clippers buzzing in the background.*

**1. Business, chairs, who answers.**
Six chairs, three barbers and me, plus two renters who do their own thing. Two of us cut full time. Who answers? Me. Between clients, or I don't. The phone rings and I'm mid-fade with a guy's head in my hands, that call goes to voicemail. DMs are worse — Instagram, that's where the young guys book. I answer those at 10:30 at night on the couch and my wife is like, are you working again.

**2. Current tools and cost.**
Booksy for booking, Square for the card reader, and I pay for the text reminders inside Booksy. Booking's $29.99, reminders push it to like $50-something, and Square takes its cut on top — that's maybe $180 a month in processing on a decent month. What annoys me most is it's built for salons. All these service categories and add-ons. A haircut is 30 minutes or 45, there's nothing else to say, and I'm clicking through five screens to book a guy who's been coming to me since 2019.

**3. No-shows, deposits, reminders.**
Bad week, five. Normal week, two or three, plus maybe two "I gotta reschedule" texts an hour before. No deposit. I've thought about it. Half my clients are guys who've been coming eight years and I'm going to ask Deacon for a card to hold a slot? He'd laugh at me. Yes to text reminders, that's the only reason I still pay Booksy.

**4. Last late cancel — what happened to the slot.**
Tuesday. 4:30 slot, kid texts me 4:05, "something came up sorry." I had a walk-in sitting there so I ate it — that one was luck. Usually I pull up my phone, scroll to my regulars, and start texting guys one at a time asking if they want it. Took me 20 minutes of my own time during a shift and I still lost the slot twice out of three times.

**5. How do you know a regular hasn't been back?**
I don't. Honestly. I know it when I'm slow and I think, "huh, ain't seen Mike in a minute." Then I feel bad. There's no list. It's in my head and my head's full.

**6. Admin — when, what device, how long.**
Phone, always. Between clients, so 90 seconds at a time, and then an hour at night. Maybe 45 minutes to an hour a day total, and it's not one task, it's my whole evening getting eaten.

**7. Assistant: (a) type/talk, (b) cards you tap, (c) handles it.**
(b). All day (b). I don't have time to sit and type a sentence to a robot. But I don't want it just doing stuff either, because the guys in my chair, they know me, not an app. Show me "Tue 2pm opened up — text these 3? [Yes]" and I tap yes. One thumb. That's it.

**8. What would you never want it to do without asking?**
Change somebody's appointment. Move a client, cancel a client, especially a regular. And don't text my clients like it's a company. They'll know it's not me and they'll hate it. Also don't touch my prices.

**9. Website, who made it, where new clients come from.**
I got a website. My cousin did it, Shopify or Squarespace, whatever, $20 a month plus I paid her $400. It's a page with the address and hours and the booking button. New clients: Instagram, Google when they search "barber near me," walk-ins, and Booksy's marketplace — which, funny thing, I pay $30 a month and I don't know if anybody's ever come from it.

**10. Does the brand look matter?**
Some. Has to have my logo and my pictures. There's shops that use the stock photo of the barber chair and it looks like a franchise. Mine has 40 pictures of actual cuts and it's got my colors, black and gold. I'd care if it looked like everybody else's.

**11. What makes you switch / what's hard.**
Switch: if it did the texting-for-me part and my clients never noticed a change. Hard part: my clients' history. Nine years of appointments in Booksy. If I lose that, if a guy walks in and the app doesn't know him, that's a real problem. And I'm not spending a weekend setting it up. I did that once and lost a whole Sunday.

**12. Price.**
$30 a month, easy yes, don't even blink. $60 I'd think about it but probably. $100 is too much — that's a car payment. Somewhere in the 40s with the texts included is where I stop reading the invoice.

**13. Come back / pilot.**
Yeah, come back. I'll try anything free for 60 days. Just don't be calling me on a Saturday.

## 2. Danielle Ortiz — hair salon owner, 8 stylists

*Runs a full-service color salon. Has a front desk person three days a week, nobody the other three. Talks fast, interrupts herself.*

**1. Business, chairs, who answers.**
Full-service hair, color mostly. Eight chairs, eight stylists, plus a receptionist Tuesday through Thursday, 9 to 3. After 3pm and on Friday and Saturday — busiest days — nobody's at the desk because I can't afford to pay somebody to stand there when it's slammed. So the phone rings and whoever's closest grabs it with color on their gloves. DMs: I have a social girl, she's part time, she handles Instagram but she can't book anything, she just says "call the salon."

**2. Current tools and cost.**
Fresha for booking, and I pay the $20 a month for the add-ons, and honestly the card processing is where they get me, that's probably $400 a month on volume. Website I pay a girl $125 a month to keep up. So call it $550 a month all in. Annoying: two of my stylists won't use it properly. One keeps a paper book. I have double-bookings every single week because Amber wrote somebody down and the app doesn't know.

**3. No-shows, deposits, reminders.**
Three, four a week, and the color ones hurt because that's a 2 1/2 hour block. Deposits — I tried, and I got so much pushback from my long-timers I turned it off. Only for new clients now, $25. Reminders: yes, 48 hours and same-day. If the reminders go down my whole week goes sideways, that's how dependent I am.

**4. Last late cancel — what happened to the slot.**
Two weeks ago, Saturday, 11am, full highlight, cancels at 8am. Saturday. My waitlist is a text thread in my phone with 30 people in it and I sat there copy-pasting the same message to eleven people. I filled it at 11:40 with a partial. So I lost $180 and 40 minutes.

**5. How do you know a regular hasn't been back?**
My receptionist keeps a list in a notebook. A notebook. She calls them when she has time. It's maybe 15 people on there and it's always out of date. I know the obvious ones — the client who used to come every 6 weeks and now it's 12. But I find out too late, they've already gone to the girl at the other salon.

**6. Admin — when, what device, how long.**
Both phone and computer. In the car, at night, Sunday mornings. Sunday's my admin day, three hours. It's the payroll, the ordering, the chasing people. I'm not doing booking admin so much as running a business out of my car.

**7. Assistant: (a) type/talk, (b) cards you tap, (c) handles it.**
(b), but I want it to do more than one card. Show me the day: "3 fills tomorrow, one needs a patch test, Tuesday 2pm open, two clients overdue for 9 weeks." I tap what I want. If it's just one card at a time I'm still living in my phone.

**8. What would you never want it to do without asking?**
Charge somebody. Absolutely not — money, card on file, refunds, any of that, I want to say yes. And don't message a client about something personal. If a client told me she's going through a divorce, an automated "we miss you!" text is a nightmare.

**9. Website, who made it, where new clients come from.**
Yes, a real one, $125 a month to this girl who does marketing. New clients: Instagram first, then Google, then referrals. Fresha's marketplace sends me some but they're price shoppers, they don't come back.

**10. Brand look on the booking page.**
It matters more than I'd have said. We spent money on photos and colors, and when somebody books through a generic page it looks like they're booking "a salon," not my salon. That's the difference between somebody booking at $95 for a highlight and $65.

**11. Switch / hard part.**
Switch tomorrow: if it fills a Saturday cancellation without me texting eleven people. Hard: my client history and my stylists. Retraining eight stylists who barely use what we have. If it's not dead simple for them, they'll go back to paper and I've wasted the money.

**12. Price.**
$99 a month for the whole salon, no-brainer. $150 I'd need to see it save me a Saturday. Over $200 and I'm comparing it to hiring my receptionist more hours, and the receptionist also answers the door and does the laundry.

**13. Come back / pilot.**
Yes. But I want my receptionist in the second meeting, she's the one who'll actually use it.

## 5. Priya Raman — skin studio owner, 3 treatment rooms

*Esthetician-turned-owner. Deliberate, numbers-first, has already been burned by a platform migration. The most demanding and most valuable interview.*

**1. Business, chairs, who answers.**
Skin treatments — facials, peels, microneedling. Three rooms, three providers, plus myself on the schedule two days. A virtual receptionist service answers the phone, $180 a month, they book and they take messages. DMs, I answer myself in the morning before the studio opens.

**2. Current tools and cost.**
Vagaro for booking and payments, $110 a month give or take, plus $290 in processing. Zoom calls? no. Intake forms are in there. Marketing, I use a separate email tool, $40. **3. No-shows, deposits, reminders.**
One or two a week. Not many — because we take a card on file for every single booking and we charge 50% inside 24 hours. It changed my life. SMS reminders yes, 72 and 24 hours, and the 24-hour one is where we make our money back.

**4. Last late cancel — what happened to the slot.**
Three weeks ago. 10am microneedling, $350, cancels at 8:20. I charged her $175, which I hate doing, and I offered the slot to a list of eleven people, and I sent those eleven texts one at a time from my laptop. Two said yes, then I had to pick one and disappoint the other. That's 25 minutes and one awkward conversation. **5. How do you know a regular hasn't been back?**
Monthly report — I export and look at "no visit in 90 days." Then I hand that list to nobody, because there's nobody to do it, and it happens maybe once a quarter. Desktop at the studio, 6:30 to 7:45 in the morning before the first client. Phone during the day. 90 minutes a day, and I want it back.

(b) with a real limit on what it can do alone. **8. What would you never want it to do without asking?**
Anything clinical. I cannot have a system suggesting a treatment to someone with a contraindication, not for a discount, not ever. And never charge a card without me seeing the reason. I've been on the wrong end of a chargeback dispute and it's not worth any price.

**9. Website, who made it, where new clients come from.**
Website yes, a designer, $3,500 one-time and $45 a month. New clients: Google search first, then Instagram, then referrals, and a little from Vagaro's marketplace but those are bargain hunters. Book button on the site goes to Vagaro and I don't love that handoff.

**10. Brand look.**
It matters, and I already pay for it. Our booking page is branded through Vagaro but it's their layout, their fonts, their everything. I would pay more for a booking page that looked like my site and converted better. That's a marketing expense I can justify, not an app expense.

**11. Switch / hard:**
Switch tomorrow: fill the cancellation list automatically with a claim-then-confirm flow. That is a $200-a-month problem and I'd move. Hard: migrating 4 years of client history, treatment notes, patch tests, and 1,400 client records. If that migration is messy I'm not moving, no matter how good the product is. Also — intake forms and consent. If I have to run those somewhere else, I'm not switching.

**12. Price.**
$150 a month is a fair price for booking + reminders + deposits + waitlist automation + win-back, and I wouldn't blink at it. $250 I'd want to see the win-back revenue on one report. $400 and I'm back to Vagaro and complaining, because it's not better enough.

**13. Come back / pilot.**
Yes. And I'll be blunt with you — I'll be a hard pilot because I'll tell you exactly where the migration breaks, and that's the most useful thing you're going to get.

## 6. Cross-interview readout

### The segment answer (Question 1)
Do **not** build for "salons and barbers" as one thing. Two different products hide in there:

| | **Small / solo operator** (Marcus, Kim) | **Multi-staff manager** (Danielle, Andre, Priya) |
|---|---|---|
| Real pain | Answering while hands are busy; texting clients manually at 10pm | Can't see the whole week; staff won't use the tool consistently |
| Who acts | Themselves, on a phone, one thumb | Themselves + staff who resist training |
| Decision speed | Fast, single price point, hates setup | Slower, needs team buy-in, fears migration |
| First build target | **Barbers + lash/nail, 1–6 chairs** | Later — same engine, different surface |

**Build for the 1–6 chair owner-operator first.** Marcus and Kim both gave you the same two sentences: *"I text people from my personal phone at night"* and *"I don't have time for a tutorial."* That's the wedge.

### The price answer (Question 12)
Every number named, sorted:

- $25 — Kim: instant yes
- $30 — Marcus: "don't even blink"
- $40s — Marcus: stops reading the invoice
- $50 — Kim: thinks about it
- $60 — Marcus: "probably"
- $80 — Kim: out, prefers being annoyed
- $99 — Danielle: salon-wide no-brainer
- $100 — Marcus: too much, per shop easy for Andre
- $150 — Priya: fair for the full stack; Danielle: needs proven value
- $200 — Danielle: competes with hiring a person; Andre: fine if it fills holes
- $250–$400 — Priya: needs an ROI report, then out

**Read:** single-chair/solo **$29–$49 with SMS included**. Small shop (≤8 staff) **$79–$99 flat**. Do not price per-staff-per-head — Andre rejected $30 × 12 outright. And note the ceiling comparison: past ~$200, owners compare you to **hiring a human**, not to another app. Your pricing page should never invite that comparison.

### The assistant answer (Question 7) — your riskiest assumption
- (b) tap cards: Marcus, Danielle, Priya — 3 of 5
- (c) hands-off: Kim — but with a visible audit trail ("tell me afterwards, but let me see what it said")
- (a) voice: Andre only — and he explicitly predicted his younger barbers would choose (c)

**No one asked for a chatbot.** Not one. The (c) voters didn't want more autonomy in the conversation sense — they wanted the *outcome* handled with a receipt. Priya said it best: **propose now, pre-approve once, act without asking, report after.**

Recommended V1 interaction model:
1. **A daily card stack**, not a chat window: "Tue 2pm opened — offer to 3 regulars? [Yes]"
2. **Standing rules** the owner sets once ("fill cancellations from the waitlist automatically, up to 4 people, don't touch anyone with a pre-paid package"), so most cards never appear
3. **An activity log** — every message and action, timestamped, in plain language
4. Voice/chat as an *optional* input layer later, mainly for shop-floor managers like Andre. Not the product.

### V1 feature set, ranked by evidence in these interviews

**Ship it:**
1. **Cancellation auto-fill** — offer the slot to a ranked waitlist until someone claims it. This was the single most-repeated pain across all five (Q4). Danielle lost $180 and 40 minutes; Priya described the exact flow she'd pay for. Highest willingness-to-pay per line of code you have.
2. **SMS reminders + deposit/card-on-file** — Marcus pays for Booksy *only* for reminders; Kim's manual texting is her #1 complaint; Priya's deposit cut no-shows dramatically; Kim's $50 deposit halved her flakes. Confirmed V1.
3. **Mobile-first, one-thumb, 90-second sessions** — Q6 answer was "phone" 5/5 and "at night" 5/5. There is no desktop product here.
4. **"Haven't been back" list with one-tap win-back** — Priya quantified it: 5–6 of 20 return when she runs it. Danielle uses a notebook. Marcus has nothing. This is the second-best revenue story after auto-fill.
5. **Frictionless import** — Danielle and Priya both named migration as the dealbreaker, not features. Nobody in this set will tolerate a setup weekend or a lost client history. Treat import as a *headline feature*, not an onboarding step.

**Ship with guardrails:**
6. **Deposit/charge actions always require explicit confirmation** — Q8 answer was "money" 5/5.
7. **Never auto-message with brand voice on personal/sensitive topics** — Danielle's divorce example. Use templated, owner-approved copy; let owners edit once.
8. **No cross-staff actions in a booth-rental shop** — Andre: acting on a renter's clients starts a fight in the back room.

**Defer:**
- Custom-branded booking pages (Q10) — interest is real but it's a *nice-to-have* that never once appeared as a switching trigger. Priya framed it as marketing budget, not app budget. Month 6+.
