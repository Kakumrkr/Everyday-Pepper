# ADR-0067: Payments: Stripe Connect Express onboarding, account status and signed, idempotent webhooks

- **Status:** Accepted. Approved by the user at the S65 brief on 2026-11-20 ("go with your recommendations on all four … don't compromise for anything"): D-1 S65 before S64/S58a/S57/S58b/S60 ([PCR-013](../plan/changes/PCR-013.md)); D-2 the backend and sandbox here, the screen in S65.2; D-3 the owner alone connects payments, managers read the status; D-4 no Stripe SDK: a small fetch adapter and our own signature check.
- **Date:** 2026-11-20
- **Builds on:** ADR-0065 (a provider behind a port, `off|sandbox|<provider>`, a signed webhook deduplicated by the provider's event id), ADR-0013 (the permission matrix), ADR-0010 (RLS, definer functions), ADR-0024 (the outbox, for the owner's push), ADR-0053/0054 (export and deletion)

## Context
Phase 15's promise is that no-shows cost the owner nothing and money never moves without the owner's OK. Every later payment (a deposit, a card on file, a no-show fee) lands in the business's own Stripe account, so the first step is to connect one: the owner goes through Stripe's own onboarding (identity, bank account, terms: no such data passes through Pepper), and Pepper knows at every moment whether the account can take payments and what Stripe still needs.

Stripe tells Pepper about changes by webhook. A webhook is outside input from the internet: it can be forged, replayed, delivered twice, delivered late or out of order, and it names a Stripe account, not a Pepper business. Each of these must be harmless.

## Decision
1. **A payments module behind a port.** `@pepper/payments` (L2, `packages/modules/payments`) owns the `PaymentsProvider` port (create an account, create an onboarding link, create an Express dashboard link, read an account), the Stripe adapter, the sandbox, the signature check, the status rules and the services. `PAYMENTS_PROVIDER` is `off` (the default: the routes are 404), `sandbox` (nothing leaves the machine) or `stripe`. With `stripe`, `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` (`sk_` or restricted `rk_`, test or live) and `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (`whsec_…`, the Connect endpoint's) are required, and a **live** key is refused unless `STRIPE_LIVE=on`: going live waits for the LLC (P1-5) and is a deliberate act, never a pasted key.
2. **No SDK: a fetch adapter pinned to one API version.** `Stripe-Version: 2026-11-18.endive` on every call, Basic auth with the key, form-encoded bodies, a 15 s timeout, an `Idempotency-Key` on every POST, every answer validated with Zod and never logged (it echoes the business's details). Stripe's error shape (`error.type`, `error.code`) maps to one `PaymentsProviderError` (retryable or not). A version bump is its own change, with the contract fixtures re-read. This keeps the dependency surface to what we use and the behaviour visible in our tests (the same choice as Twilio, ADR-0065).
3. **Express through `controller` properties** (Stripe's current form; `type=express` is deprecated): `controller[stripe_dashboard][type]=express`, `controller[fees][payer]=application`, `controller[losses][payments]=application`, `controller[requirement_collection]=stripe`, `country=US`, `capabilities[card_payments][requested]=true`, `capabilities[transfers][requested]=true`, and `metadata[pepper_tenant]` (an id for reconciliation, never trusted). Stripe collects and verifies the owner's identity and bank details on its own pages; the owner manages payouts in the Express dashboard. **Consequence accepted:** with the Express dashboard the platform pays Stripe's fees and carries negative balances (refunds and disputes beyond the balance). S66–S68 recover fees with an application fee and keep losses small (deposits, not full prices; S68's receipts for disputes).
4. **One account per business, created once.** `payment_accounts` holds at most one row per business (unique `tenant_id`) and the Stripe account id (unique). A row made with another provider or mode (the sandbox, or test keys before going live) reads as `not_connected` and connecting again replaces it with the next generation, as a disconnected row is: an account is only ever used by the deployment that made it. `POST /v1/payments/onboarding` (owner only, `payments.manage`) reads the row; with none, it creates the account with `Idempotency-Key: pepper-account-<tenant id>` (two taps, a retry or two devices within Stripe's 24 h window get the same account) and inserts it on conflict do nothing, keeping the row that won. It answers a fresh single-use **onboarding link** (`account_onboarding`; `collection_options[fields]=eventually_due` so the owner finishes everything in one go) with `return_url` `…/more/payments?stripe=return` and `refresh_url` `…/more/payments?stripe=refresh`; links are never stored (they live about five minutes). `POST /v1/payments/dashboard` answers an Express dashboard login link once details are submitted. No external call runs inside a database transaction.
5. **Status is derived, from a fresh read, never from an event's body.** The row keeps what Stripe says: `charges_enabled`, `payouts_enabled`, `details_submitted`, `requirements.currently_due`/`past_due`/`eventually_due`/`pending_verification` (codes only, at most 100 each), `current_deadline`, `disabled_reason`, and `synced_at` (when Pepper read it). `paymentAccountStatus` (contracts, pure) answers one of: `not_connected` (no row, or disconnected), `onboarding` (details not submitted), `rejected` (`disabled_reason` `rejected.*`), `action_needed` (anything currently or past due, or disabled for another reason), `pending` (Stripe is verifying), `ready` (charges and payouts on, nothing due). `GET /v1/payments/account` (owners and managers, `payments.read`) answers the status, the flags, the codes due with the deadline, and `canManage`. `POST /v1/payments/account/sync` (owner, rate-limited) reads the account again: the return page uses it, since a webhook can lag the redirect.
6. **The webhook: signature first, then once per event, then a fresh read.** `POST /v1/webhooks/stripe` (no session, no Origin check, 404 while off):
   1. **Signature.** The `Stripe-Signature` header is parsed as comma-separated `key=value` pairs in any order (unknown keys ignored; `v0` and the unsigned debug entries never used). `HMAC-SHA256(secret, "<t>.<raw body>")` in hex must equal **any** `v1` (so a secret being rolled keeps working), compared in constant time, and `t` must be within **300 s** of now in either direction. Anything else: 401 `webhook_signature_invalid`, nothing read. The raw bytes are Nest's `rawBody`; the JSON is parsed only after the check.
   2. **Shape and mode.** The event is validated (`id` `evt_…`, `type`, `created`, `livemode`, `account`); a `livemode` that isn't the key's is acknowledged and ignored (a misconfigured endpoint must not change a business), as is an unhandled type.
   3. **Once per event.** `payment_webhook_events` (platform table, forced RLS, reachable only in the payments-webhook context and by logged operators) records each handled event id once (insert on conflict do nothing) with its outcome. A redelivery of a `processed` or `ignored` event answers 200 and changes nothing; one left `received` (an earlier attempt failed) is processed again; a failure answers 500 so Stripe retries (for up to three days). Rows are deleted after 30 days by the daily clean-up.
   4. **Which business.** `payment_account_tenant(provider, account)` (a definer function: the tenant id for a stored account id, nothing else; a business being deleted is left out) is the only route from an event to a business; the event's metadata and body are never trusted for it. An unknown account is acknowledged and recorded `ignored`. An event that arrives in the moment between Stripe creating the account and Pepper storing it is ignored the same way; the owner's return page reads the account again (`…/sync`), so nothing waits on it.
   5. **What changes.** `account.updated` and `capability.updated` read the account again from Stripe and apply it with `syncPaymentAccount`, which updates only if this read is newer than the stored `synced_at` (the time is taken before the call), so two reads that finish out of order never move the status back. Two deliveries of one event at once may both handle it; every effect is idempotent for that reason. An account Stripe refuses for good to show is recorded `ignored` (`unreadable_account`), not retried. `account.application.deauthorized` (signed by Stripe, the account can no longer be read) marks the row `deauthorized_at`; the status is `not_connected` and onboarding again creates a new account.
   6. Every status change is audited (`payments.account_status_changed`, the statuses before and after; no personal data), in the same transaction.
7. **The owner hears about it (top-up).** When a sync moves the status to `ready` or `action_needed`/`rejected`, every owner of the business gets one push in the same transaction ("Payments are ready" / "Stripe needs one more thing" with the deadline as a date in the business's time zone, opening More → Payments), deduplicated by account, status and deadline, so a redelivery or a second read pushes nothing twice.
8. **A sandbox for tests and S65.2's journeys.** With `PAYMENTS_PROVIDER=sandbox` where the scripted model may run (CI, or local outside production), `SandboxPaymentsProvider` keeps accounts in Redis for a day (memory in unit tests), its onboarding link goes straight to the return page, and `POST /v1/dev/payments/account` (`{ state: 'onboarding' | 'pending' | 'action_needed' | 'ready' | 'rejected' | 'deauthorized' }`, the signed-in owner's business) sets the sandbox account and delivers a **signed** event through the same webhook handler, so the journey exercises the real verification and dedupe path. Anywhere else it is 404.
9. **Permissions** (ADR-0013, additive): `payments.manage` (owner only: it decides where the money goes) and `payments.read` (owner and manager). Staff see nothing of payments.
10. **Export and deletion.** `payment_accounts` is in the business's export (`payments.json`: the Stripe account, the status, when connected). Deleting a business deletes its row and its webhook events (both cascade from the business); the Stripe account stays the owner's at Stripe (Pepper can't close it for them), which the deletion screen says in S65.2.

## Consequences
- Forged, replayed, stale, duplicated, late and out-of-order webhooks are each harmless and each has a test; the business is found only through the stored account id.
- Pepper never sees a card, a bank account or an identity document: Stripe's pages collect them.
- The platform carries Express's fees and negative-balance liability (§3); S66–S68 design deposits and refunds with that in mind.
- Nothing is tried against real Stripe until the user opens a test-mode account: the adapter is coded from Stripe's documentation (read 2026-11-20) and tested on fixtures in its documented shapes. S66 confirms it in test mode.
