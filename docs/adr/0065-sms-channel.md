# ADR-0065: The SMS channel: Twilio behind a port, consent and opt-out decided by the domain

- **Status:** Accepted. Approved by the user at the S61 brief on 2026-11-12 ("let's start with your recommendation"): Twilio, the S61/S61.2 split, recorded fixtures plus a local sandbox.
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Builds on:** ADR-0003 and ADR-0024 (the transactional outbox), ADR-0026 (a second channel on the outbox), ADR-0040 §7 (opt-out), ADR-0063 §3 (a signed webhook that only records), [PCR-011](../plan/changes/PCR-011.md) (Phase 14 first, S61 split)

## Context
Owners expect booking reminders and confirmations by text; every competitor sends them, and a text is read when an email isn't. A text to a US mobile is regulated: the TCPA needs prior express consent for automated texts, the carriers' A2P 10DLC rules need opt-in proof and working STOP/HELP, the FCC's 2025 rule makes an opt-out by "any reasonable means" binding, and several states (Florida's being the strictest) limit the hours. Every text also costs money per segment, and one curly quote turns a 160-character GSM-7 text into a 70-character UCS-2 one, so the same message costs three times as much.

Phase 14 is built in sandbox mode first (P1-5): live sending needs the LLC and 10DLC registration (S64).

## Decision
1. **Twilio behind a port.** Core holds the `SmsSender` port (`send({ to, body, id, statusCallbackUrl }) → { providerMessageId, segments }`). The Twilio adapter (`TwilioSmsSender`, runtime) posts form-encoded to `/2010-04-01/Accounts/{sid}/Messages.json` with Basic auth. It sends from a Messaging Service (`MG…`, preferred: a number pool, the 10DLC campaign and Twilio's opt-out list come with it) or from one number. Its answers are validated, and an error is classified by Twilio's code: 21610 (the recipient replied STOP to Twilio) and the other bad-recipient codes are permanent; 429, 5xx and network failures retry. The error's code is kept, never its message. Twilio has no idempotency key for messages, so the limit ADR-0024 names for email holds: a worker that dies after Twilio accepted a text but before it is marked sent sends it again after the lease, and so does a call that timed out (15 s) after Twilio accepted it. Pepper prefers a rare duplicate reminder to a lost one. Nothing after the acceptance can fail on Pepper's side: the segments are kept in range before they are recorded. `SMS_PROVIDER` chooses `off` (the default; queued texts fail as an unconfigured channel, and S62's callers check first), `sandbox` (`SandboxSmsSender`: keeps the last 100 in memory, logs a masked line, never leaves the machine) or `twilio`. Twilio's own test credentials work with the `twilio` adapter. The adapter is coded from Twilio's documentation and tested on recorded fixtures in its documented shapes; S64 confirms it live.
2. **SMS is an outbox channel.** The outbox gains the type `sms`, with content `{ to, body, urgent? }` sealed like email. `to` is a US or Canadian mobile in E.164 form (`+1`). `queueSms` runs in the business change's transaction like `queueEmail`. Its text is normalised to GSM-7 where a plain character means the same (curly quotes, dashes, the ellipsis, non-breaking spaces), counted in segments (160/153 GSM-7, 70/67 UCS-2) and refused above three. A sent text is metered as `sms_segments`.
3. **The domain decides who may be texted, at send time.** Every delivery checks, in this order:
   1. **Opt-out.** The number replied STOP and hasn't replied START since: the text is dropped (`SmsOptedOut`).
   2. **Consent.** The business has an active consent record for the number: when, how (`booking_page`, `member_settings`) and which wording (`SMS_CONSENT_WORDING`, versioned and never edited in place). With none, or a revoked one, the text is dropped (`SmsNoConsent`).
   3. **Quiet hours.** Outside 8:00–20:00 in the business's time zone, a text waits for the next 8:00, and the wait doesn't use one of its 8 attempts. `queueSms` already schedules into the window, and the delivery check catches a retry that crossed 20:00. `urgent` (a time-critical note about today's appointment, set by domain code only) skips the hours, never consent or opt-out.

   Callers ask `smsPermission(tx, phone)` first, to fall back to email (S62). Delivery checks again, because a STOP can arrive between queueing and sending.
4. **Opt-out is per number, for all of Pepper.** V1 sends every business's texts from Pepper's one sender, so a STOP to it means "no more texts from this number". `sms_keyword_events` is a platform table (forced RLS, no delete). It holds one row per signed keyword message (provider, the provider's message id unique, the phone in E.164, `stop`/`start`/`help`, when), plus a `stop` row when Twilio refuses a send with 21610 (the person replied STOP to Twilio directly). A number is opted out when its latest `stop`/`start` row is `stop`. Delivery asks `sms_opted_out(phone)`, a definer function that returns a boolean and nothing else. The rows outlive the businesses: the person asked not to be texted. Phone numbers are stored as they are, like `email_suppressions` addresses: an HMAC would break the list if the outbox secret were ever rotated.
5. **Keywords are handled in the webhook's own request.** `POST /v1/webhooks/sms` (Twilio's incoming-message webhook) checks `X-Twilio-Signature` (HMAC-SHA1 over the public URL and the sorted form fields, compared in constant time) and the account id before anything is read. It then classifies the text:
   - **STOP:** `STOP`, `STOPALL`, `UNSUBSCRIBE`, `CANCEL`, `END`, `QUIT`, `OPTOUT`, `OPT OUT`, `REVOKE`, alone or opening a short message ("Stop texting me"), any case, punctuation ignored;
   - **START:** `START`, `UNSTOP`, `YES`;
   - **HELP:** `HELP`, `INFO`.

   The event is recorded and the reply goes back as TwiML in the same response. So a STOP is honoured before Twilio has its answer, and the next delivery is blocked. A replay (the same message id) changes nothing and gets no second reply. Any other text gets no reply and isn't stored: two-way texting is V1.1, and S62 adds "C" to confirm. `CANCEL` stays an opt-out word, so S62 must not use it for cancelling a booking, and S63's approve-by-text uses `Y`, never `YES`. Off (404) until `SMS_PROVIDER=twilio` with `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN`. Rate-limited like the Resend webhook. Neither the phone nor the text is logged.
6. **Delivery status by a signed URL.** A text sent with a known `AUTH_BASE_URL` asks Twilio to call `POST /v1/webhooks/sms/status?t=<business>&m=<message>`. The signature covers the URL, so the business and the message can't be forged, and no lookup across businesses is needed. `sms_deliveries` (tenant) holds the provider's message id, the status and its error code. It is written when the text is marked sent, and moved by callbacks only forwards (queued → sending → sent → a final state), because callbacks arrive out of order. Twilio reports 21610 when the send is refused, so the opt-out is recorded at send time (§4). The delivery row keeps no number, so a callback's error code is only recorded.
7. **Records.** `sms_consents` is tenant data, the business's TCPA proof. It is in the export, removed with the business, and audited as `sms.consent_given` and `sms.consent_revoked` (ids only). At most one consent per number is active.

## Consequences
- S62 (reminders, confirm by text), S63 (approve by text) and S64 (`send_client_message` over SMS) add callers only; the channel's rules are in one place and tested once.
- A STOP is honoured immediately, and a text never goes to a number without the business's consent record, even if a caller forgets to ask.
- One sender for every business keeps 10DLC to one brand and campaign (Pepper's, as the ISV, S64). The cost is that a STOP stops every business's texts to that person. Since S61.2 the replies name the businesses the number said yes to (below).
- Before S64 goes live, the Messaging Service's own STOP/HELP auto-replies are configured so the person gets one confirmation, not two (STATUS "Waiting on the user" 5).

## Built in S61.2 (consent on screen)
- **The booking page's box** (AC-X-64, AC-X-65): unticked, shown under the phone field once a number is typed, the current wording with the business's name, linked to the "Text messages" sections of the terms and privacy pages (carriers' terms; the number never shared for marketing). The request carries the wording version shown (`smsConsent`); a ticked box needs a US or Canadian number. The version waits on the booking request (`booking_verifications.sms_consent_version`, migration 0076) and becomes a consent only when the booking is confirmed, in its transaction, for the number typed and the client booked.
- **The profile** (AC-X-66, AC-X-67): `GET /v1/clients/:id/texts` answers on / opted out / stopped / never, from every consent of the client in force (on while any number is in force and not opted out), with the words as shown; `POST …/texts/stop` (owners and managers) revokes them all, audited as the member. No "turn back on": only the client can, by ticking the box again. START ends a STOP but gives no consent.
- **Replies name the business** (AC-X-68): `sms_consent_businesses(phone)`, a definer function answering at most two names (the latest consent first; revoked consents and businesses being deleted left out) and nothing else, read in the webhook's request. "{names} via Pepper:"; a name in GSM-7 only, cut at 40 characters, so a reply stays in two GSM-7 segments.
- **SMS's AI disclosure** (AC-X-69, ADR-0064 for texts): every queued text says who wrote its words (`wordsBy`, required); `ai` text must end with "Written with AI help: {app}/privacy#ai" (`smsWithAiDisclosure`), counted in the segments.
- **For S62:** the phone on the booking page is not verified (only the email is), so S62 sends an opt-in confirmation text on each new consent (who, how often, STOP/HELP: the carriers' recommended first message), and reminders go to the number on the client's consent in force, not `clients.phone` (a returning client keeps the business's details).

## Built in S62 (reminders and confirm by text)
Approved at the S62 brief ("good with your recommendations… revise and improvise"): D-1 the screens in S62.2; D-2 the day before plus a same-day text 2 h ahead; D-3 a hashed link per reminder; D-4 settings on `tenants`, this section instead of a new ADR. Migration 0077.
- **Two reminder kinds** (AC-X-70): `booking_reminders` holds one row per booking and kind. The day before is due 24 h ahead, by text or else email. The same day is due at the business's lead (1, 2 or 3 h), by text only. Both are planned in the booking's transaction and kept out of the night (before 8:00 → 8:00, from 20:00 → 19:30, in the business's zone), so a text is rarely held by the quiet hours. A same-day text must fall on the start's own day and at least 30 minutes before it. A booking made under 24 h ahead still gets the same-day text. One made under the lead gets none, because its confirmation has just gone.
- **Text first, email as the fallback** (AC-X-71). Under the booking's lock, the job takes the reminder and asks `smsPermission` for the number on the client's consent in force (never `clients.phone`, which isn't verified). It texts when allowed, the deployment sends texts (`SMS_PROVIDER` set) and the business has texts on. Otherwise the day-before reminder is emailed and the same-day one dropped. Dedupe keys are per booking, kind and start.
- **Words** (AC-X-72, `texts.ts`, `wordsBy: template`): "{business} via Pepper:", the service and staff member, and the date written out (never "tomorrow", since a text may wait). The text asks "Reply C to confirm" until the client has. The client's first reminder from the business ends with "Reply STOP to opt out." (S62 top-up). Typed names go through `smsPlainText` (GSM-7 only, cut), so every text fits three GSM-7 segments with the longest names (tested).
- **A link in every reminder.** `booking_manage_links` stores another hashed token per reminder, and the booking page's manage lookup accepts either. As with the confirmation's link, it changes a booking only before it starts. The clean-up removes links 30 days after their booking's start. The email reminder carries one too.
- **Opt-in and confirmation texts** (AC-X-73). A new consent from the booking page gets the carriers' first message with the booking ("You're booked…", the link, what, how often, rates, HELP, STOP). The staff name is dropped there so the required words fit. A later confirmation for a client whose texts are on gets one short line. Both are queued by the api's notify in the booking's transaction and follow the quiet hours.
- **"C" to confirm** (AC-X-74). `classifySmsReply` reads "C"/"Confirm" alone, after the keywords, so an opt-out always wins (CANCEL included). The webhook asks appointments' `confirmBookingByText` (core stays L1). `sms_confirm_target(phone, at)`, a definer function, answers ids only: the soonest upcoming confirmed booking whose reminder went to that number in the 48 h before, to a client whose consent in force is for it, at the start it was reminded for, leaving out businesses being deleted. That business's transaction checks the booking under its lock, sets `bookings.client_confirmed_at` once (audited `booking.client_confirmed`, no number) and answers with the time. A second "C" or a replay changes nothing and is told "already confirmed". After a STOP the "C" still counts, but gets no reply.
- **Settings** (AC-X-75): `tenants.reminder_settings` (`texts`, `dayBefore`, `sameDay`, `sameDayLeadHours`), `GET /v1/reminders/settings` for every member (`canChange`), `PATCH` for owners and managers (`settings.manage`, audited `reminders.settings_updated`). A change re-plans upcoming confirmed bookings in the same transaction; a kind turned off is also dropped at its due time.
- **The last text** (AC-X-76, top-up): `booking_texts` records what each queued text was about (its client, booking, kind, start; no number). `GET /v1/clients/:id/texts` answers `lastText` (waiting until when, sent, delivered, not delivered, not sent and why) from the outbox message and the delivery report. The profile line is S62.2's.

## Built in S79 (waitlist offers by text)
Approved at the S79 brief ("Start with your recommendations and complete the session successfully"): D-1 the team records a client's yes (`staff_recorded`) and the first text confirms it; D-2 a text instead of the email; D-3 one session; D-4 this section. Migration 0093. AC-X-220…228.
- **A third wording, its own sources** (AC-X-220). `sms-consent-v3` names waitlist openings only. It is recorded as `waitlist_join` (the booking page's "Join the waitlist" box) or `staff_recorded` (the team's "They agreed to texts about openings", audited as the member, never from the AI: `addToWaitlistIn` refuses an AI actor, and `add_to_waitlist` has no such argument). One consent is in force per number and source, so a v3 consent stands beside the client's v1. `SMS_SOURCE_WORDINGS` says which versions each source records; `recordSmsConsent` and the check `sms_consents_source_wording` refuse others, and each request schema accepts only its box's version (`smsConsentVersionFor`). The verification row takes v1 with a booking, v3 with a waitlist join.
- **Consent per purpose** (AC-X-221). `smsPermission(tx, phone, purpose)` looks for an active consent whose version is in `SMS_PURPOSE_WORDINGS[purpose]`: `appointments` (v1, v2) or `waitlist_offer` (v3). Queued texts carry `purpose`, and delivery checks it again, so a v3 number gets no reminder and a v1 number no offer. A STOP stops both (opt-outs are by number).
- **Offers by text** (AC-X-224…226). `waitlistCandidatesIn` keeps a client without an email when `offerTextNumber` finds a v3 number the business may text, the deployment and the business have texts on, and the first sendable minute (`smsSendableAt`) leaves at least `WAITLIST_TEXT_MIN_OPEN_MINUTES` (15) before the offer closes. `send_waitlist_offers` texts such a client instead of emailing them (`queueWaitlistOfferText`, key `waitlist.offer.sms:<offer id>`). The text is held for the undo-send grace like the emails and handed to the runner (`scope.heldMessage`), so Undo takes it back. It carries `validUntil` (the offer's close): delivery drops it after that (`SmsExpired`). A regular's offer is never texted (marketing needs its own opt-in and campaign). An offer that can't be texted in time goes by email, or isn't made without an address.
- **How long it is open** (AC-X-227, top-up). The text says "Open for 2 hours, until 3:15 PM", counted from when it can go; the emails say "The offer is open for 2 hours, until …", from when it was made.
- **The opt-in text** (AC-X-223). After a waitlist yes, one text names who, what, how often, rates, HELP and STOP (`waitlist.opt_in.sms:<entry id>`), through `BookingEmails.waitlistOptIn`.
- **Known limits.** A phone-only client who claims an offer gets no confirmation text (v3 doesn't cover confirmations) and has no email: the claim page's "You're booked" and its manage link are their record. Taken notices stay email only. Anyone can type someone else's number in the public box, as with the booking box: the opt-in text with STOP is the safeguard. A client's profile names only the latest consent's purpose: a client with both a reminders yes and an openings yes sees one line.
