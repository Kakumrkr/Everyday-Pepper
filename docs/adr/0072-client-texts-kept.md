# ADR-0072: A client's text is kept by the one business it fits, and answered from a card

- **Status:** Accepted. Approved by the user at the S80 brief on 2026-11-27 ("Sure, start with your recommendation and complete the session successfully"): D-1 one session; D-2 route by booking texts, waitlist offers sent by text and replies; D-3 a 7-day window; D-4 a neutral reply that names no business, once a day; D-5 Reply needs a v1 or v3 consent in force, owners and managers only, 300 characters, Undo until sent.
- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Builds on:** ADR-0065 (the SMS channel: one Pepper number, consent per purpose, quiet hours, STOP for all of Pepper), ADR-0066 (approve by text: a member's "Y"/"N"), ADR-0039 (the feed), ADR-0043 (cards first), ADR-0010 (RLS, forced), ADR-0025 (the clean-up). Phase 18's rules: [PCR-017](../plan/changes/PCR-017.md).

## Context
Before S80 a client's text to Pepper's number that wasn't a keyword ("Running 10 late", "Can I move to 3?") was neither stored nor answered (ADR-0065 §5: two-way texting later). Owners lost the message and the client got no answer. Every business shares the one number, so a text carries no business: it must be routed, and a wrong route would show one business's client to another. S81 will read texts with a model; S80 is the foundation, with no model.

## Decision
1. **Order kept.** The webhook handles STOP, START and HELP first (`handleInboundSms`), then "C" (confirm), then "Y"/"N" with a code, and a member's bare "Y"/"N" gets its "how to reply" answer. Only what is left is a client's own text. A client's bare "Y" or "No" (not a member's number) is now kept as their text.
2. **Routing by an id-only definer function.** `sms_reply_target(phone, at)` (migration 0094, `SECURITY DEFINER`, `search_path` pinned, execute granted to `pepper_app` only) answers `(tenant_id, client_id)`. A business fits when, in the 7 days before the text (`CLIENT_TEXT_WINDOW_DAYS`), it sent that number a booking text (`booking_texts`), a waitlist offer by text (`waitlist_offers.texted_at`, set when the offer text is queued) or a reply (`client_texts`, `out`), to a client whose consent in force (not revoked) is for that number. A business being deleted is left out. The function answers only when exactly one (business, client) pair fits. Two businesses, or two clients of one business sharing the number, answer nothing. It reads through `TO CURRENT_USER` lookup policies, like `sms_confirm_target`.
3. **None or two fit: stored nowhere.** The text is not kept anywhere. The number gets one neutral reply, "Pepper: We couldn't tell which business this text is for, so it wasn't passed on. Please contact the business directly. Reply STOP to opt out.", at most once a day (a Redis counter keyed by an HMAC of the number), and only when some business on Pepper has a consent in force for the number. Never after a STOP, never to a member's number, and none when Redis is unreachable (fail closed: the text is stored nowhere either way).
4. **Kept in `client_texts`.** A tenant table with forced RLS (`tenant_isolation`, no member scope: the service reads the client through `findClient` first, so staff reach only their own clients, as for `booking_texts`). It holds the client, the direction (`in`: the provider and its message id, unique per business, so a replay is kept once; `out`: the reply's outbox message and the member), the words (1–1600 characters) and when. It is in the export (`client-texts.json`). Forgetting a client deletes their texts (`deleteClientRow`; the row is anonymised, so the cascade alone wouldn't). A merge moves them to the kept client. The clean-up deletes texts 90 days after they came (`CLIENT_TEXT_RETENTION_MS`), and the cards whose text is gone. Kept and replied texts are audited (`client_text.received` as the system; `client_text.replied` and `client_text.reply_taken_back` as the member) with ids only, never the words or the number.
5. **One card a client a day.** The kept text writes a `client_text` feed item: audience none, so only owners and managers see it (`member_scope`). One per client and local day (`client_text:client:<id>:<day>`). A later text that day updates it and opens it again, even after Done or a dismissal. Its booking is the client's next confirmed visit (the top-up: "Likely about their next visit: Thu, Nov 5, 2:00 pm for Haircut with Jess"). The payload holds ids and counts only; the words are read with the card from `client_texts`, so they go when the text goes. It is an informing kind: it shows in the quiet start and while suggestions are paused. Home shows it in its own section, "Texts from clients", with the quote as written (plain text, never inside Pepper's sentences), Reply, "Open {client}" and Done.
6. **Reply is the member's own words, by text.** `POST /v1/clients/:clientId/texts/replies` (`clients.manage`: owners and managers) queues "{business} via Pepper: {words}" (at most 300 characters, `wordsBy: 'member'`) under a new purpose, `reply`. A reply may go on either client consent (v1 or v3, `SMS_PURPOSE_WORDINGS.reply`): the client started the conversation (CTIA's conversational messaging), so no new consent wording is needed. Delivery checks it again like every text (STOP drops it, quiet hours hold it). It is held 10 seconds (`CLIENT_TEXT_REPLY_HOLD_SECONDS`) so `DELETE …/replies/:replyId` can take it back. Undo cancels the outbox message, removes the reply and opens the card again, or answers `too_late`. Refusals: 409 `texts_off` (the deployment's or the business's texts off), 409 `client_not_textable` (no consent in force, or STOP), 422 `reply_too_long` (the words and the name don't fit three segments, e.g. emoji), 404. At most 60 replies an hour a business.
7. **No model, no chat tool change.** The chat's `get_feed` never shows `client_text` (`feedForAiIn` leaves it out), so every chat tool's output and the agent's instructions are unchanged and nothing is re-recorded. Reading texts is S81's (`read_client_text`), answering them S82's.
8. **The profile.** `GET /v1/clients/:id/texts` adds the client's texts and replies (the last 20, newest first), `canReply`, and (the carried top-up) every consent in force with its purpose ("Appointment texts", "Waitlist openings"), number, words, when given and whether the number said STOP. Before, it showed only the latest.

## Consequences
- A client who books with two businesses and texts both is answered by neither until one texts them again alone. That is the safe failure: a text never reaches a business it may not belong to. S81 may ask the client which business they mean.
- The 7-day window means a text a week after the last reminder gets the neutral reply. Replies keep a conversation inside the window.
- A reply under a waitlist (v3) consent is an answer to the client, not an offer. The terms ask businesses to keep replies to what the client asked, never marketing.
- `sms_reply_target` scans three sources by time. Each has a time index (booking texts by client and time, client texts by business and time). Waitlist offers are few per business and are swept when they close.
- The privacy page and terms say where a text goes, for how long, and what happens when no business fits.
